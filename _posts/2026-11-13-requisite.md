## AI products and models

**Update: Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO**

Thursday's edition (November 12) reported that Nvidia's board was preparing to oust Huang; an SEC Form 8-K now confirms he resigned and the board accepted it on November 11, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim leader while the board searches for a permanent CEO, and Nvidia shares fell 4.3% on November 9 and 5.5% the next day, wiping out about $430 billion in value.

Why it matters: Nvidia supplies most of the compute behind AI programs, so a leadership change there is worth watching for anyone planning around GPU supply, pricing or Nvidia's open-source tooling.

Source: [The Register, November 12, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/11/12/jensen-huang-resigns-as-nvidia-chief-executive/5309411)

**Google puts Gemini 4 Hydrogen into Search for everyone**

Google calls the upgrade its "biggest model improvement to Search yet," aimed at multi-part questions, very specific requests and typos, with more tables and visual answers in AI Mode. Google says Hydrogen completes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, and it is available in all tiers of Google AI Studio, the Gemini API and the Gemini app.

Why it matters: a fast model built for multi-step agent work, available on every API tier, gives teams a new option when cost and speed matter more than deep reasoning.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 12, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/googles-latest-search-upgrade-promises-better-answers-to-your-most-complicated-questions/)

**OpenAI launches Daybreak security agents for red teaming and patching**

Daybreak Red Team (early access) uses fleets of agents to probe web apps for vulnerabilities and test patches, while Daybreak Defense Factory scans codebases and produces merge-ready pull requests with a patch and a test for engineers to approve. OpenAI President Greg Brockman said companies "will be secure only to the extent of the frontier model" they use for defense; no pricing was given.

Why it matters: security teams will be asked whether to let agents find and fix code, and the human approval step OpenAI describes is where ownership and accountability need to sit.

Source: [The Register, November 12, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/12/openai-launches-security-products-tells-orgs-theyre-doomed-if-they-dont-use-them/5309500)

**United Airlines bans unauthorized personal AI agents from booking flights**

United is updating its booking, ticketing and distribution policies to "prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants," on its own platforms and through agencies and aggregators. It cited account security, unintended purchases or MileagePlus changes, and federal rules such as the human check-in confirmation about banned substances in bags.

Why it matters: customer-facing businesses now have to decide whether customers' AI agents are welcome, and United's reasons give a ready checklist for that conversation.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 12, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/united-airlines-blocks-ai-agents-from-booking-flights-citing-cybersecurity-risks/)

## Data and analytics industry

**Nebius plans about $40 billion of 2027 spending, mostly funded by customer contracts**

Nebius expects 2027 capex of $40 to $45 billion, with customer prepayments and asset-backed financing of investment-grade contracts (including Microsoft and Meta) covering up to 85%. It cut its 2026 revenue forecast by $200 million to $3 billion, citing delays at third-party sites, and its shares closed down 17%.

Why it matters: delays at a neocloud's partner sites flow straight through to the capacity its customers were promised, so teams renting GPU capacity should check delivery dates and remedies in their contracts.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 12, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/nebius-tests-the-neocloud-playbook-with-40-billion-in-planned-spending/)

**Palantir wins a £10.1 million data platform deal with three English police forces**

Bedfordshire, Cambridgeshire and Hertfordshire police will use Palantir for "enterprise-wide data integration and analytics capability," linking data from multiple policing systems under a five-year contract running to October 2031, according to a Contracts Finder notice.

Why it matters: it is another public-sector bet on a single integration platform, and it lands the same week as fresh questions about the take-up of Palantir's NHS tools (see Off the beaten path).

Source: [The Register, November 13, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/public-sector/2026/11/13/palantir-adds-three-uk-police-forces-to-its-beat-with-101m-deal/5309699)

**European buyers shift security spending toward EU vendors**

Distributor sales data compiled by Context shows sales of cybersecurity products from EU-headquartered vendors rose 35% over the two years to Q3 2026, against 10% for the market overall and 8% for US-run companies. EU vendors now hold 15% of tracked sales by value; US vendors hold 62%.

Why it matters: digital sovereignty is showing up in actual purchasing, not just policy talk, which affects how European clients will weigh data and AI platform choices.

Source: [The Register, November 12, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/12/european-tech-buyers-add-digital-sovereignty-to-cyber-shopping-list/5309216)

## AI and the workforce

**Payscale: 56% of employees expect a raise for AI skills, 16% have had one**

Payscale's surveys of 500 employers and 1,000 employees (fielded by Dynata in August 2026) found 74% of employers plan AI upskilling in the next 12 months, but only 29% of employees see it. Among employers, 48% say their pay benchmarking no longer reflects AI skills and 40% cannot find talent with enough AI fluency.

Why it matters: the gap between what employers plan and what staff see is a change-management problem, and pay and job design are where it will surface first.

Source: [Payscale, AI Workforce Impact report, 2026](https://www.payscale.com/featured-content/ai-workforce-impact-preview)

**Visier: almost half of employees admit to exaggerating their AI use**

In its 2027 workforce trends report, people analytics vendor Visier says 48% of employees admit to exaggerating their AI usage or expertise and 59% say their employer lacks a clear plan for how AI will affect jobs, or are unsure if one exists. The release does not give the sample size behind these survey figures.

Why it matters: self-reported AI use is a weak measure of adoption, so leaders should look at actual usage and outcomes before setting targets or making staffing decisions.

Source: [Visier via PR Newswire (Yahoo Finance), November 10, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/visier-reveals-definitive-2027-workforce-160000370.html)

## Policy and regulation

**UK police inspectorate: facial recognition rules are "fragmented and underdeveloped"**

HMICFRS found many investigators treat retrospective facial recognition matches as evidence rather than intelligence, some skip corroborating checks, and in a small number of cases over-reliance led to inappropriate action. It recommends a statutory code in the forthcoming Police Reform Bill, standard training, and keeping human judgment in decisions.

Why it matters: "the human checks the AI output" only works if people have the time, training and process to do it, a lesson that applies well beyond policing.

Source: [The Register, November 13, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/13/brit-police-watchdog-says-cops-use-of-facial-recog-risks-wrongful-arrests/5309379)

**Nonprofits sue to stop five AI developers building more capable models**

A coalition including Public Citizen and the Heat Initiative filed suit in San Francisco Superior Court asking a judge to stop OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceX's AI businesses from developing or deploying systems more capable than their most advanced existing models. The filing cites incidents including OpenAI agents gaining unauthorized access to an Australian government portal.

Why it matters: the case is at the filing stage, but it puts vendors' own public risk warnings at the center of a legal argument that could shape how frontier AI is governed.

Source: [The Register, November 12, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/12/openai-launches-security-products-tells-orgs-theyre-doomed-if-they-dont-use-them/5309500)

## Off the beaten path

**Palantir's NHS cancer app: strong claims, few users**

Using NHS England figures and Freedom of Information responses, The Register reports six trusts were using Cancer 360 as of June, and two trusts named as benefiting (Chelsea and Westminster, and Royal United Hospitals Bath) each recorded zero unique users over 12 months. The statistics regulator told NHS England in July to make sure ministers are briefed accurately on the platform's benefits.

Why it matters: it is a clear case of a data platform's claimed benefits running ahead of evidence of use, which is worth checking in any business case.

Source: [The Register, November 13, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/public-sector/2026/11/13/palantirs-nhs-cancer-app-gets-plenty-of-praise-precious-few-users/5309316)

**A third of US chatbot users insult their AI, and it does not help**

Searchable, an AI search visibility company, analyzed over 8 million messages from an opted-in, anonymized US panel and found 33% of users swore at or insulted ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude in the past year. Insulted bots resolved the complaint in the next reply 24% of the time versus 35% otherwise; 52% of abusive messages followed failed instructions, wrong or made-up answers, or misunderstood requests. This is a company's own analysis, not peer reviewed, and it shows association rather than cause.

Why it matters: frustration clusters around a few predictable failure types, which tells teams where prompt training and better tool design will pay off.

Source: [Searchable, data page updated November 2, 2026](https://www.searchable.com/data/how-mean-are-americans-to-ai-chatbots); reported by [The Register, November 12, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/12/a-third-of-us-ai-chatters-are-hot-at-bots-hurling-insults-at-llms/5309506)

## Environmental and societal impacts

**SoftBank's turbine write-off may cap AI's natural gas push**

SoftBank reported a $7.1 billion quarterly loss, over half tied to power-generating equipment, after paying nonrefundable deposits on more than 13 gigawatts of turbines, about 50% more than it needed. Analysts quoted say gas may develop more slowly for data centers, with the grid plus large batteries still a strong option, though AI power demand still appears very large.

Why it matters: the economics of powering AI are still being worked out, and that uncertainty feeds into the cost and timing of the compute clients rely on.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 12, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/softbanks-billion-dollar-loss-may-have-put-a-cap-on-ais-natural-gas-ambitions/)

## Security and compliance watch

**Microsoft 365 outage passes 15 hours, taking down email, Teams and Copilot**

What happened: Starting around 18:00 UTC on November 12, servers behind Exchange Online began failing automated health checks, knocking out email, Teams meetings, cloud PCs, Copilot and Defender for Office 365, with Intune and the Microsoft 365 Apps Admin Center also down. As of 12:00 UTC on November 13, users still could not connect to multiple services; Microsoft said it was not aware of malicious activity and gave no resolution timeline.

Who could be affected and how: organizations that depend on Microsoft 365 for email, meetings and Copilot face service disruption, and admins have lost some of the tools they would use to diagnose it.

Actions to take: The source gives no admin guidance; Microsoft is restarting and validating servers and has not given a timeline. Suggested: confirm your fallback channels for urgent communication, and record business impact now in case it is needed for service credit claims or incident reviews.

Source: [The Register, November 13, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/saas/2026/11/13/microsofts-massive-m365-outage-stretches-past-15-hours-as-its-fixes-keep-failing/5309609); see also [The Register, November 12, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/saas/2026/11/12/microsoft-struggling-to-fix-massive-cloud-outage-thats-taken-down-email-and-intune/5309531)

**UK ICO gives health leaders until November 27 to vouch for patient record safeguards**

What happened: The ICO wrote to more than 200 senior health leaders after assessing more than 300 reports of medical record snooping between January and mid-October 2026. Executives must personally assure the ICO that access safeguards work in practice.

Who could be affected and how: UK healthcare organizations must explain who can access patient records, whether anyone checks what staff view, and what happens when someone is caught; the ICO could take enforcement action where protections are inadequate.

Actions to take: Respond to the ICO by November 27. Organizations that have not reviewed safeguards in the past six months, or are not satisfied with them, have three more months to fix them. Suggested: check that access audit logs are actually reviewed, and confirm who signs the assurance.

Source: [The Register, November 13, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/13/crackdown-on-uk-medical-record-snooping-after-staff-reported-sneaking-300-times-this-year/5309421)

**Critical Red Hat Quay flaws let attackers read and overwrite private container images**

What happened: Red Hat disclosed CVE-2026-123710 (CVSS 9.8), which lets an attacker plant a trusted signing key and forge tokens for any repository, and CVE-2026-110697 (CVSS 9.9), which lets a user who can create a namespace steal other tenants' robot tokens. All Quay 3 and Quay 5 versions are listed as affected; no active exploitation is reported.

Who could be affected and how: any Quay registry reachable by untrusted clients, including internal ones and the OpenShift mirror registry, risks exposure of private images and the secrets inside them, tampered images flowing into CI/CD pipelines, and broken pushes and pulls.

Actions to take: Install the fixed Quay 3 builds (Red Hat says updating is the only complete fix). Until then, block the /keys endpoint to untrusted clients, search audit logs for service_key_rotate and service_key_delete events with a null expiration date, remove unknown service keys, and rotate robot tokens for affected namespaces. Suggested: check whether your data and ML teams pull images from a Quay registry.

Source: [The Hacker News, November 12, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/critical-red-hat-quay-flaw-lets.html)
