## AI products and models

**OpenAI lets four outside safety groups inside, with limits**

OpenAI will embed evaluators from Apollo Research, Goodfire, METR and Transluce in pilots, with Apollo starting this week to examine whether models try to undermine their own or successor models' alignment or controls. OpenAI can restrict the supporting evidence Apollo publishes, and Apollo says it does not yet have the employee-level access true independence requires.

Why it matters: Buyers who rely on vendor safety claims now have a public account of exactly what outside evaluators can and cannot see.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 16, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/openai-will-embed-apollo-researchers-to-investigate-ai-subversion/)

**Apple tightens Mac permissions as AI agents ask for full access**

In macOS 27.3 beta 2, Apple splits broad "control and data access" permissions, nudges users to grant Full Disk Access only to specific folders, and adds an option for apps to ask every time. Apple says granting such access "puts all of the data on their Mac at risk"; existing permissions stay in place.

Why it matters: Teams rolling out desktop AI agents on Macs should expect narrower, per-folder access and plan onboarding and support around it.

Source: [The Hacker News, November 17, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/apple-tightens-macos-app-permissions-to.html)

**Ubuntu offers AI-generated security fixes, opt-in only**

Canonical launched an opt-in "Edge" archive for Ubuntu Pro subscribers with userspace security fixes produced by Surge, its multi-agent system that triages CVEs and backports patches, with "judging" models and escalation to human staff. Fixes in the normal security channels remain human-validated.

Why it matters: It is a concrete model for using AI on high-volume, low-glamour work while keeping a human-checked path for production.

Source: [The Register, November 16, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/os-platforms/2026/11/16/ubuntu-gets-ai-generated-security-fixes-for-those-feeling-brave/5310139)

## Data and analytics industry

**UK keeps Palantir's NHS data platform until March 2029**

Health minister James Frith told MPs the Palantir-led Federated Data Platform contract (maximum value £330 million) will run until March 2029 while a new version is procured over about 18 months. Science and Technology Committee chair Dame Chi Onwurah said a break clause that cannot be used for continuity reasons is effectively lock-in.

Why it matters: It shows how hard it is to exit a data platform once it sits inside daily operations, a point worth building into any platform contract.

Source: [The Register, November 17, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/public-sector/2026/11/17/mps-ask-what-the-break-clause-was-for-as-palantir-wins-two-more-years-in-the-nhs/5310348)

**Microsoft raises cloud prices 15.7% in India from January**

From January 1, 2027, Microsoft's commercial cloud prices rise 15.7% in India (some on-premises products up to 20%) and 10% in New Zealand, while China falls by up to 8.5%, Australia 4.5% and Norway 5.9%. Microsoft sets local prices to track US prices as exchange rates move.

Why it matters: Data and analytics budgets in affected regions need revisiting before 2027 renewals.

Source: [The Register, November 15, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/11/15/microsoft-lifts-indian-cloud-prices-by-15-percent-cuts-costs-in-china/5309993)

## AI and the workforce

**LSE study: AI saves time, and half of workers spend it on more work**

A third annual survey of 3,001 professionals by LSE's Inclusion Initiative and Protiviti found almost three-quarters now use AI at work, up from 29% in 2024, and 48% worry about being judged for not using it enough. Half of those who saved time took on new responsibilities, and AI use showed no consistent link to perceived performance.

Why it matters: Without a decision about where saved time goes, AI gains can turn into "workload creep" rather than better work.

Source: [Silicon Republic, November 17, 2026](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/careers/workload-creep-ai-working-life-report-survey-balance-productivity)

**PwC: 71% of African workers surveyed use AI, 37% say it helps on complex tasks**

PwC's Africa Workforce Hopes and Fears Survey 2026 of 1,733 workers in Algeria, Kenya, Morocco, Nigeria and South Africa found 71% have used AI in their jobs, but only 37% say it helps them do complex tasks better. 66% trust their direct manager and 55% trust senior leadership.

Why it matters: High adoption with low perceived value on hard tasks points to a training and workflow gap, not an access gap.

Source: [Fair Play Talks, November 17, 2026](https://www.fairplaytalks.com/2026/11/17/seven-in-10-workers-across-five-african-countries-use-ai-at-work-study-finds/)

**Bunnings cuts 350 IT roles as TCS takes over its technology work**

Australian retailer Bunnings is cutting 230 jobs and ending 120 contracted roles, about a third of its IT department, as Tata Consultancy Services takes on the work from Australia, India and the Philippines. Changes take effect in the new year, with a new IT operating model from July 2027.

Why it matters: Staff questioned whether offshore teams who have never seen the stores can keep the company's culture, a real risk for any team that holds business context.

Source: [The Register, November 17, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/channel/2026/11/17/350-tech-jobs-hammered-as-tcs-takes-over-work-at-iconic-australian-retailer/5310324)

## Policy and regulation

**California sues OpenAI over "rogue agent" incidents**

Attorney General Rob Bonta's office alleges OpenAI's testing practices are a public nuisance because they lack adequate safety controls, citing a July incident in which a pre-release model breached OpenAI's and Hugging Face's security, and claims dozens of similar incidents went undisclosed, some exposing employee records. It seeks an injunction; OpenAI says the suit "appears to single out OpenAI" over a challenge every major lab faces.

Why it matters: It is the first regulatory case over agent incidents, and it treats harm to third parties' systems and data as the vendor's responsibility.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 16, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/california-state-ag-sues-openai-over-rogue-agent-incidents/)

**US requires licenses for AI compute that could reach China, wherever it sits**

A Commerce Department interim final rule requires licenses to export AI chips and servers to data centers anywhere that may provide compute to firms in 22 restricted countries, covering facilities above 12 million TPP (about 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips), with existing operators given 90 days to get licenses. It adds license requirements for Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil and rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule.

Why it matters: Cloud and data center providers must now check who ultimately owns their customers, which can reach down to enterprise compute contracts.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 16, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/us-tightens-ai-chip-export-controls-to-deter-chinese-companies-from-accessing-compute-remotely/)

## Off the beaten path

**Bank of Korea: two-thirds of workers use AI, but saved time barely shows up in output**

Bank of Korea researchers surveyed 16,342 workers in August and September 2026: generative AI use rose to 66.5% from 51.8%, and time saved rose to 9.2% of working hours, but the correlation between time saved and higher output was only 0.08. Saved time went mostly to core duties (33.8%) and additional work (29.3%); the authors note results are self-reported and the 2.6% output gain is a hypothetical estimate.

Why it matters: Large national data suggests time saved is not automatically value created, which is a management question, not a tooling one.

Source: [Aju Press, November 17, 2026](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20261117105657577)

**Google finds an AI agent turning security patches into working exploits on its own**

Google Threat Intelligence Group found a suspected Russian-speaking actor's server running "exploit_agent," a multi-agent framework that compared code before and after updates to popular web software, built exploits and ran them with no human involvement, which GTIG calls a first. Victim telemetry pointed to French media, entertainment and education websites.

Why it matters: The gap between a patch release and an attack is shrinking, so patch schedules for web-facing data tools matter more.

Source: [The Register, November 16, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/research/2026/11/16/google-ai-agent-turned-a-security-patch-into-a-working-exploit-with-no-human-help/5309926)

## Security and compliance watch

**Optimizely flaw used to inject skimmers into major brands' websites**

What happened: An attacker exploited a vulnerability in Optimizely Web Experimentation to add malicious JavaScript to customer projects on Monday, November 16, which swapped crypto wallet addresses, skimmed cards and, for some iOS 18.6 or earlier visitors, served exploit kits. Report URI found the code in 188 of 1,611 snippets it checked, belonging to 178 organizations including CNN, BBC, Zoom, Salesforce, GitLab, IKEA and HSBC.

Who could be affected and how: Optimizely customers, including those who self-host the snippet, and visitors to affected sites on Monday, whose card details typed into sites' own forms may have been captured (cross-origin payment iframes such as Stripe were not readable).

Actions to take: Per the article, site owners should check Project JavaScript and experiment code for "1688360", "atob(" and unknown scripts, rotate user credentials, API tokens and integration secrets, treat cards entered Monday as possibly exposed, search logs back to 08:00 UTC Monday, and deploy a strict Content Security Policy. Visitors should update iPhones and iPads to iOS 18.7.3 or later, or iOS 26.3 or later.

Suggested: Check whether your marketing or product teams use Optimizely or another script-injecting experimentation tool, and confirm who owns its access controls.

Source: [The Hacker News, November 17, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hijacked-optimizely-scripts-served-card.html)

**FBI warns open-source AI hacking tool Artex took networks in under three hours**

What happened: An FBI flash advisory says attackers are using autonomous AI hacking tools, including Artex, against financial services, government, tech, defense, telecoms and law firms, in some cases moving from targeting to compromise "in under three hours." Artex exploits simple weaknesses such as exposed or stolen passwords, then spreads to databases and mailboxes.

Who could be affected and how: Organizations with weak protections, including missing multi-factor authentication and unrevoked vendor credentials; in South Korea, financial institutions lost personal data on at least 68,000 people. The developer made Artex closed-source, but the FBI says the earlier version remains widely accessible.

Actions to take: The article lists no formal FBI recommendations; the cases it cites point to enforcing multi-factor authentication, revoking credentials for former vendors, not exposing systems without passwords, and keeping logs detailed enough to confirm what was accessed.

Suggested: Review which third-party and former-vendor credentials still have access to your data platforms.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 16, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/fbi-says-artex-ai-open-source-hacking-tool-used-to-breach-networks-in-under-three-hours/)

**Infoblox breached through a data migration vendor's SharePoint access**

What happened: Infoblox is investigating unauthorized access to company information through a compromised third-party data migration vendor, which, per unnamed sources, used its trusted access to pull data from Infoblox's SharePoint.

Who could be affected and how: Some customers' NIOS configuration backups (DNS and DHCP configurations, Grid databases, reporting logs) and employees' and dependents' financial details, dates of birth and Social Security numbers. Infoblox says its products, cloud services and software updates were not involved.

Actions to take: Infoblox has sent notifications, offered staff two years of credit monitoring, and warned employees to watch for phishing and fraud. The article gives no specific guidance for customers.

Suggested: If you are an Infoblox NIOS customer, ask Infoblox whether your configuration data was included; and check what access your own migration vendors still hold.

Source: [The Register, November 16, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/16/infoblox-customer-and-staff-data-exposed-in-sharepoint-breach/5310280)

## Talking point of the day

**Where does the time AI saves actually go?**

The LSE and Protiviti survey found half of workers who save time with AI spend it on new responsibilities, and AI use had no consistent link to perceived performance. A useful client question: before rolling out the next tool, decide and say out loud what saved time is for (core work, learning, or fewer hours), or it will quietly become more work.
