## AI products and models

**ChatGPT's Chat and Work now share one usage meter**

OpenAI is merging the Chat and Work experiences into one interface with a single usage limit, rolling out to a small group of Pro subscribers from November 24 and to more users in December. Instant model conversations stay unlimited, and OpenAI says only a small fraction of the heaviest users may hit limits sooner.

Why it matters: Teams that lean on ChatGPT for long agentic work should check whether heavy users will run into the shared cap.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 25, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/openai-says-heavy-chatgpt-users-may-hit-limits-sooner-as-it-merges-chat-and-work/)

**OpenAI pilots Private Inference for sensitive business work**

Sam Altman says OpenAI is piloting Private Inference with its first enterprise customers, running queries on isolated hardware that OpenAI says it cannot access and whose security can be independently verified. The claims come from OpenAI and are not independently checked; the offering is business-only and in limited pilot.

Why it matters: For regulated data that has stayed out of AI tools, this is a new option worth tracking, but buyers will want the independent verification OpenAI promises.

Source: [The Decoder, November 25, 2026](https://the-decoder.com/openai-wants-companies-to-trust-it-with-sensitive-work-by-promising-not-to-look-at-their-data/)

**Ant Group releases Ling-3.2-air, an open multimodal model for office workflows**

Ant Group published the weights for Ling-3.2-air under an MIT license, a text, image and video model with 124 billion total parameters (5.5 billion active per token) aimed at tool-using agents. On Ant's own AutomationBench-AA test, task completion rose to 61% from 15.7% for its predecessor.

Why it matters: Permissively licensed models aimed at document and office work give data teams more self-hosted options, though the headline result is from Ant's own benchmark.

Source: [TechNode, November 25, 2026](https://technode.com/2026/11/25/ant-releases-ling-3-2-air-multimodal-model-under-mit-license/)

## Data and analytics industry

**UK ICO reprimands the Home Office over a border watchlist that could not fix bad data**

The ICO found that operators of Helios, the border watchlist system launched in October 2024, could not correct inaccurate records; a temporary fix has since received more than 6,500 correction requests and removed about 5,000 records. The ICO said the failings risked people being wrongly held for extra checks, and about 800 people were delayed at the border pending checks, though the Home Office could not say how many were due to Helios.

Why it matters: It is a clear example of a regulator treating the ability to correct data as a launch requirement, not a later fix.

Source: [The Register, November 26, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/26/uk-home-office-waved-through-border-system-without-a-working-fix-for-bad-data/5312162)

## AI and the workforce

**Anthropic study: AI is cutting hiring more than jobs, and review is the bottleneck**

In 66 interviews at 58 US companies (all Anthropic customers, chosen for range rather than at random), only two companies reported AI fully eliminating a job, five pushed out staff who would not adopt AI, and about two-thirds said they are hiring less. Interviewees named review volume, expert resistance and uneven speed-ups as obstacles, and the strongest results came from reorganizing roles and shared data; the authors say findings are qualitative and self-reported.

Why it matters: The pressure on people shows up first in fewer hires, cut contractors and overloaded reviewers, which is where change plans need attention.

Source: [Anthropic, November 23, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai)

## Policy and regulation

**42 attorneys general fight Character.AI's plan to auction users' private chats**

Character.AI, in Chapter 11 bankruptcy since October, plans to auction chat histories and other personal data from more than 21 million US accounts, including over 2.1 million belonging to minors, on December 1. A coalition of 42 state attorneys general is intervening, arguing the company told users their data would not be sold and that a sale could breach chatbot safety laws taking effect January 1, 2027.

Why it matters: It shows that what users type into AI tools can become a sellable asset when a vendor fails, so contract terms on data in bankruptcy matter.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 25, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/state-ags-fight-character-ais-plan-to-auction-off-users-private-chats/)

**Nvidia halts server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries after new US cloud rules**

New US export controls aim to stop Chinese firms renting AI compute from foreign cloud providers, giving Southeast Asian providers 90 days to apply for a license. The Financial Times reports Nvidia told partners including Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, and banks have halted about $500 million in loans to Singapore cloud provider Aolani.

Why it matters: Where AI compute can legally run is shifting quickly, which affects cloud choices for organizations with workloads in the region.

Source: [The Decoder, November 26, 2026](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-to-malaysia-singapore-thailand-and-indonesia-after-tighter-us-export-controls/)

**Australia passes a Digital Duty of Care law with fines up to 5% of global revenue**

Australia's Parliament passed the Digital Duty of Care bill on November 25, replacing the Online Safety Act 2021 with a duty on online service providers to provide a "safe online environment" and to remove harmful content quickly. Breaches can bring fines of up to 5% of global revenue, and the law takes effect one year after passage.

Why it matters: Platforms serving Australian users have about a year to show their product design meets a broad safety duty.

Source: [Silicon Republic, November 25, 2026](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/business/blow-for-big-tech-as-australias-digital-duty-of-care-bill-passes)

## Off the beaten path

**How Anthropic used Claude agents to rewrite its Messages API in Rust**

Anthropic's engineering write-up says Claude drafted the core request path in about a week, but verification took months: production traffic was shadowed through both versions, and the match rate rose from about 24% in April to 99.6% by August, with engineers reviewing every fix. The company reports about 70% less CPU and over 80% less memory per request; these are its own unverified figures.

Why it matters: It is a detailed, practical account of where human review still sits when agents write most of the code.

Source: [Anthropic, November 25, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust)

## Environmental and societal impacts

**FCC approves SpaceX's plan for up to 1 million AI data center satellites**

The FCC authorized SpaceX's Starmind constellation of up to 1 million satellites to process AI workloads in orbit, with the full build due by 2040, while declining to do a full environmental assessment. It denied a waiver on end-of-life disposal, and the American Astronomical Society warned of significant interference with ground-based astronomy.

Why it matters: The AI buildout is moving into orbit with limited environmental review, which raises new questions about who weighs its wider costs.

Source: [Gizmodo, November 25, 2026](https://gizmodo.com/feds-approve-spacexs-ai-data-center-satellites-all-1-million-of-them-2000845526)

**Tidal will delete more than 29 million fully AI-generated songs**

Tidal says more than 43% of tracks uploaded in the past 90 days were detected as AI-generated, and it will remove fully AI-made songs from December 9 while still accepting AI-assisted music. About 27 million tracks, 6.3% of the catalog, are fully AI-generated, and 96% of those have no streams.

Why it matters: It is one of the clearest numbers yet on how much AI content is flooding a platform, and on the cost of filtering it.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 24, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/tidal-to-ban-and-remove-millions-of-ai-generated-tracks-as-ai-slop-inundates-music-streaming/)

## Security and compliance watch

**1Password breached through an employee's GitHub account**

What happened: An attacker used an employee's corporate GitHub account to copy source code from private repositories, reach the CI/CD system and push malicious code to backend services, and took administrative data for a subset of customers, including names, emails, account identifiers, public keys and SRP parameters. 1Password says vault contents, passwords and recovery keys were not accessed.

Who could be affected and how: A small number of 1Password customers, who have been notified individually; 1Password says leaked invitation and recovery tokens cannot be used alone and warns of possible targeted phishing.

Actions to take: 1Password says customers do not need to act but should watch for unexpected phishing; it has revoked the stolen token, rotated internal credentials and lengthened invitation and recovery codes from six digits to ten characters.

Suggested: Warn staff who use 1Password to treat unexpected 1Password emails with caution, and review how your own developers' GitHub accounts and CI/CD tokens are protected.

Source: [The Hacker News, November 26, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/1password-hacked-customer-data-and.html)

**FinCEN takes some systems offline after a cyber intrusion, extends filing deadlines**

What happened: The US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network reported unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems and took some offline as a precaution. It has not said what was accessed, when it began or who was behind it.

Who could be affected and how: Financial institutions filing Bank Secrecy Act reports, including those using Secure Direct Transfer Mode, plus companies and individuals with beneficial ownership or FinCEN Identifier filings due in the window; some filing services may be unavailable.

Actions to take: Under order FIN-2026-R002, covered reports due November 18 through December 29 can be filed until December 30. All other BSA obligations, including suspicious activity monitoring, still apply, and urgent information can go to the Financial Institutions Hotline at 1-866-556-3974.

Suggested: Confirm with your compliance team which filings fall in the relief window and log any failed submissions.

Source: [The Hacker News, November 25, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/us-treasurys-fincen-takes-some-systems.html)

**24 Check Point management servers compromised in a suspected AI-driven campaign**

What happened: GreyNoise says an unattributed actor exploited CVE-2026-16232 in Check Point Security Management servers between October 17 and November 2, stealing firewall databases and certificate authority files and disabling security policies. GreyNoise suspects, but has not confirmed, that agentic AI tools drove the scanning and exploitation.

Who could be affected and how: Organizations running Check Point Security Management servers, with 22 organizations affected so far across finance, energy, government, transport and manufacturing in about 16 countries.

Actions to take: Check Point says customers should apply the available updates for CVE-2026-16232 and follow its hardening guidance to restrict management access and spot suspicious activity.

Suggested: Confirm the patch is in place on every Check Point management server and check that management interfaces are not reachable from the internet.

Source: [The Hacker News, November 25, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-compromise-24-check-point.html)

## Talking point of the day

**Your AI bottleneck is probably your reviewers**

Anthropic's interviews at 58 companies found that review volume overwhelming scarce human attention was a key obstacle, and that faster coding often outpaced code review, sales and decisions. For clients, the angle is to map who checks AI output today, how long each check takes and where work queues up, then redesign roles around those points rather than adding more tools. The authors stress the findings are qualitative, so it is a framework to test, not a benchmark.

Source: [Anthropic, November 23, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai)
