## AI products and models

**Update: Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 a share and raises $81.3 billion**

Since the November 11 edition covered its $70 to $78 price range, Anthropic has priced its shares at $83 and sold 980 million shares, up from 825 million planned, for $81.3 billion, ahead of SpaceX's $75 billion. TechCrunch puts the fully diluted valuation at about $1.5 trillion, with Amazon owning nearly 15% and Google almost 14%.

Why it matters: As a listed company, Anthropic will report its revenue, losses and strategy in public filings, so clients who depend on Claude gain a regular view of their vendor.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 18, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-reportedly-prices-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-projected-range/)

**Google adds Neon, a Gemini 4 model built for long-running agent work**

Google describes Neon as "engineered for long-running agent workflows"; it can work alone or orchestrate parallel Hydrogen subagents in Google Antigravity. It costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, and scores 61 on Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index, just ahead of Carbon at 60.

Why it matters: Teams building agents now have four Gemini 4 models with overlapping roles, and The Register notes the names do not signal a clear hierarchy, so model choice and cost routing need deliberate testing.

Source: [The Register, November 19, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/19/google-continues-model-chemistry-experiment-with-neon/5310934)

## Data and analytics industry

**MongoDB buys Firebolt to put transactions and analytics in one platform**

MongoDB has acquired Firebolt, an Israeli cloud data warehouse founded in 2019, for an undisclosed price, and says Firebolt's engine will run alongside the database and stay in sync automatically. Customers will get access "in the coming months", and existing Firebolt customers keep their service.

Why it matters: MongoDB is pitching one system for operational and analytical data because AI agents need fresh data and fast queries, but The Register notes it is not yet clear whether the design avoids separate copies of the data.

Source: [The Register, November 18, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/databases/2026/11/18/mongodb-snaps-up-firebolt-to-bring-transactions-and-analytics-under-one-roof/5310809)

**Unsiloed AI raises $7.5 million to turn messy documents into agent-ready data**

The seven-person startup, backed in a seed round led by Felicis, extracts tables, charts and text from business documents into structured data and shows where each item came from so people can check it. It claims 99.78% accuracy on about 84,000 data points from 542 financial documents; these are company figures, not independently verified.

Why it matters: Document extraction with traceable sources is often the unglamorous step that decides whether an agent's answers can be trusted in finance, legal or healthcare work.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 18, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/felicis-leads-7-5m-bet-on-unsiloed-ai-to-make-enterprise-documents-usable-by-ai-agents/)

## AI and the workforce

**Nadella: Microsoft's 60,000 buyout offers were about giving staff "agency"**

At the DealBook Summit, Satya Nadella said the October buyout offer to about 60,000 employees was meant "to make sure that at least everyone felt like they had agency in making choices," and that Microsoft is not replacing workers with AI but reorganizing so everyone's job centers on managing it. He cited the 11-developer team behind Cowork, which he said builds "a software factory that builds the product," and predicted every employee will become a manager of AI agents.

Why it matters: One of the largest AI vendors is telling its own staff their jobs will shift to supervising agents, which is a role-design and training question every client will face.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 18, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)

**Goodbody: 63% of Irish jobs are highly AI-exposed, but that is "not a forecast of job losses"**

Goodbody's Irish Economy Health Check found AI and data centre investment drove about one-fifth of growth in modified domestic demand over the four quarters to Q2 2026, and that about 63% of Irish employment is in highly AI-exposed occupations (33% more likely augmented, 30% more vulnerable to replacement). It found only a weak link so far between exposure and job changes, and said more than 60% of specialist AI roles are hard to recruit for.

Why it matters: Goodbody says training, governance and workflow redesign are what turn AI efficiencies into wider productivity, which points Irish employers at skills and process work rather than headcount cuts.

Source: [Silicon Republic, November 18, 2026](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/business/ai-investment-driving-20pc-of-irish-economic-growth-says-report)

## Policy and regulation

**California proposes a state power to shut down dangerous AI models**

A Government Operations Agency report, "AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond," recommends state-set baseline safety standards, independent testing, ongoing oversight by evaluators accountable to the state, an emergency shutdown power, and power to cap AI that designs its own successor. These are recommendations, not enacted rules; the governor ordered agencies to fast-track SB 813 (certifying verification organizations) and AB 1405 (a registry of AI auditors).

Why it matters: If enacted, independent third-party evaluation would become a standing requirement for frontier developers, creating demand for audit and assurance skills.

Source: [Office of the Governor of California, November 18, 2026](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/11/18/governor-newsoms-nation-leading-ai-safety-framework-is-getting-even-stronger-as-california-releases-next-steps-on-mandatory-safety-standards-and-oversight/)

**Trump tells Xi the US opposes "globalist regulation" of AI**

At the APEC meetings in Shenzhen, President Trump said the US would not allow global AI controls, while Xi, according to Xinhua, urged the two countries to cooperate to keep AI under human control. Details of an AI incident hotline agreed in September were due to be worked out on Thursday; the outcome has not been reported.

Why it matters: Organizations operating across the US, EU and Asia should not expect a single global AI rulebook soon and will keep managing differing national regimes.

Source: [Euronews, November 19, 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/19/us-president-trump-rejects-global-ai-regulation-as-chinas-xi-calls-for-cooperation-on-safe)

## Off the beaten path

**Researchers map 22,096 uncensored AI models, and only 13% carry warnings**

Researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Nanyang Technological University analysed model metadata for 22,096 uncensored LLMs on Hugging Face, Kaggle, John Snow Labs, OpenCSG and ModelScope; only about 13% carried moderation warnings, and 27 models, downloaded about 136,000 times, showed signs such as malware signatures or unsafe deserialization. The authors note they relied on disclosed metadata, so undisclosed uncensored models may be missed, and they evaluated text output only.

Why it matters: Uncensored models also reach users through apps and routing services where people may not know which model they are using, so model provenance belongs in AI procurement and supply chain checks.

Source: [The Register, November 19, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/19/uncensored-ai-models-are-thriving-on-code-hosting-platforms/5310891)

## Environmental and societal impacts

**Climate Council: nine Australian data centres could emit up to 39 million tonnes in a decade**

The Climate Council analysed the nine largest proposed Australian data centres and estimated up to 39 million tonnes of greenhouse gases in a decade if they run mainly on fossil fuels, which one expert called a worst-case scenario. It wants rules requiring large projects to bring on new renewables and storage within three years of approval; the federal government is expected to legislate a mandatory framework early next year.

Why it matters: Where AI workloads run, and on what power, is becoming a planning condition, which affects vendor choice and the emissions numbers in clients' sustainability reporting.

Source: [ABC News, November 19, 2026](https://abc.net.au/news/2026-11-19/climate-council-report-data-centre-millions-tonnes-pollution-/107400036)

**Sierra Club: data center growth could push US power emissions back to 2011 levels**

The Sierra Club's "Artificial Intelligence, Real Pollution" report says US electric-sector CO2 emissions could reach 2.3 billion tons a year by 2030 as nearly 250 gigawatts of new gas plants are planned or built. It says tripling solar and doubling wind could cut emissions 25% below 2025 levels and lower annual electricity costs by more than $5 billion nationally.

Why it matters: The report ties AI demand to both emissions and household power bills, which is shaping the local opposition data center projects now face.

Source: [WWAY, November 17, 2026](https://www.wwaytv3.com/sierra-club-report-warns-data-center-growth-could-reverse-15-years-of-climate-progress/)

## Security and compliance watch

**CISA adds an exploited LiteLLM flaw used for LLMjacking to its must-patch list**

What happened: CISA added four exploited flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, including CVE-2026-40217 (CVSS 8.8) in BerriAI's LiteLLM, which Google Threat Intelligence Group says attackers used to turn limited inference keys into full admin credentials. The others are in Apache Axis, Vanderbilt's REDCap research data platform, and Synology DSM.

Who could be affected and how: Teams running LiteLLM as an AI gateway risk stolen model access and persistent attackers; REDCap users (common in research and health data) face unauthenticated code execution, linked to a web shell and cryptominer.

Actions to take: US federal agencies must fix the DSM, REDCap and Axis flaws by November 21, 2026 and the LiteLLM flaw by December 2, 2026. Axis 1.x is end-of-life and unpatched: migrate to another SOAP engine or apply the July 12, 2026 patch, and check whether Confluence has "Remote API (XML-RPC & SOAP)" enabled.

Suggested: Find every LiteLLM and REDCap instance in your estate, including ones data or research teams set up themselves, and rotate the provider keys LiteLLM holds after patching.

Source: [The Hacker News, November 19, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/cisa-warns-of-4-exploited-flaws-linked.html)

**Payee data exposed at about 40 Irish credit unions through a shared IT provider**

What happened: Attackers exploited a flaw in Wellington IT's online banking platform between October 2 and 4, exposing saved payee details including names, IBANs, BICs, sort codes, payment references and addresses; the flaw was closed on October 6. Passwords, PINs and logins were not affected.

Who could be affected and how: Members of about a quarter of Ireland's credit unions, whose payee records can be linked to them and used in targeted scams. Many credit unions only learned of it in recent days, and Minister of State Robert Troy called the provider's delay "not acceptable."

Actions to take: St. Canice's credit union warned members not to transfer money to anyone claiming funds must be moved to safety because of the breach.

Suggested: If you rely on a shared software provider, confirm your contract sets a notification deadline that lets you meet your own GDPR breach-reporting duties.

Source: [The Register, November 19, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/19/cyberattack-on-service-provider-exposes-payee-data-at-40-irish-credit-unions/5310979)

**N-able confirms a breach behind its email security outage in three regions**

What happened: N-able said attackers accessed a server in its Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud, which led it to disable management interfaces and APIs in the Americas, APAC and Europe; it first blamed the disruption, under investigation since November 16, on configuration issues. N-able says it has no evidence customer data, including tenant mailboxes, was compromised; the investigation continues.

Who could be affected and how: Managed service providers and their clients using these products have no access to the management portal, APIs, quarantine or logging, though mail flow and filtering still work.

Actions to take: N-able has contacted Mail Assure and SpamExperts partners directly with guidance on whether action is required; restoration was underway as of 8 a.m. UTC on November 19.

Suggested: Ask your MSP whether you use either product, and review held mail and console activity once logging returns.

Source: [The Hacker News, November 19, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/n-able-confirms-hackers-accessed-cloud.html)

## Talking point of the day

**If a customer's AI agent moves the money, who pays when it goes wrong?**

Citibank updated its online user agreement so customers who give third-party AI tools account access are responsible for what those tools do, including if the tool is hacked; JPMorgan and Bank of America announced similar policies in October, PNC changed its terms on November 18 and Citizens emailed customers the same warning. The client angle: before staff or customers connect agents to accounts, map where liability sits in each provider's terms, because the banks are deciding it now, and against the agent user.

Source: [TechCrunch, November 18, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/)
